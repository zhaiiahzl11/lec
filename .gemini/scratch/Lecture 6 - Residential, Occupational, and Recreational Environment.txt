I.  RESIDENTIAL  ENVIRONMENT  
●  "A  house  is  not  a  home  unless  it  contains  food  and  fire  for  the  mind  as  well  as  the  body."  -Benjamin  Franklin  ●  A  house  must  be  more  than  just  a  physical  shelter —in  terms  of  environmental  health,  we  view  our  residences  as  our  primary  sanctuaries.  Individuals  should  be  able  to  leave  the  pressures  of  society  behind  and  relax  in  safety.   
A.  HOUSING  AND  HEALTH  
●  Where  we  live,  the  house  or  the  neighborhood,  has  an  impact  on  our  health.  ●  Residence  refers  to  one's  house  or  living  accommodation  ○  Can  be  temporary  (e.g.,  Dormitories)  or  permanent  accommodation  ●  Quality  of  Housing  refers  to  the  physical  condition  of  an  individual's  house,  as  well  as  the  quality  of  the  social  and  physical  environment  in  which  the  house  is  located  
○  Ex.  Good  neighbors,  Status  of  the  area  in  terms  of  pollution  ●  Healthy  Housing  refers  to  a  shelter  that  supports  a  state  of  complete  physical,  mental,  and  social  well-being  (WHO).  ○  A  non-negotiable  element  in  a  healthy  community.  ○  It  means  having  proper  heat,  light,  ventilation,  and  plumbing,  all  of  which  can  directly  affect  a  person’s  emotional  and  physical  state.   
B.  BASIC  PRINCIPLES  OF  HEALTHFUL  HOUSING  
●  We  address  the  fundamental  needs  of  a  human  when  it  comes  to  housing.  
●  4  Fundamental  Pillars  ○  Meet  physiologic  needs  ■  Address  the  human  body’s  internal  department  ○  Meet  psychological  needs  ■  Mental  needs  of  a  person  ○  Protection  against  Diseases  ○  Protection  against  Injury

D.  SUBSTANDARD  HOUSING  
Definition  ●  Does  not  meet  the  standards  of  living  ○  If  the  needs  (mentioned  earlier)  are  not  met,  then  the  housing  is  substandard  ○  Poses  an  active  risk  to  the  physical  well-being  of  the  occupants  ■  A  house  that  fails  to  provide  basic  necessities.  (eg.  plumbing,  and  electricity)  Characteristics  ●  Lack  of  proper  heating,  plumbing,  electricity,  or  sanitation.  ●  Violates  the  building  code  Deterioration  ●  Deterioration:  Housing  that  is  damaged,  unmaintained,  or  structurally  unsound.  
●  Deteriorated  housing  condition  ○  Ex.  If  there  are  appliances  that  no  longer  work  or  need  replacing,  or  damage  in  the  house  due  to  old  age  or  overuse.   Health  Risks  of  Substandard  Housing  ●  Physical  Well-being:  Higher  risk  of  infectious  disease  and  respiratory  issues.  ●  Mental  Health:  Decreased  mental  health  due  to  chronic  environmental  stress.  ●  Community  Impact:  Poses  a  risk  not  just  to  occupants,  but  to  neighbors  and  visitors  as  well.   ●  Living  in  substandard  homes  will  create  a  ripple  effect  of  risk.  Lack  of  hygiene  leads  to  diseases,  and  the  constant  stress  of  living  in  an  unsafe  structure  will  degrade  the  mental  health  of  your  household  ●  In  the  most  extreme  form,  substandard  housing  is  usually  found  in  the  slums.   
E.  SLUM  
●  A  heavily  populated  urban  informal  settlement.  ●  According  to  international  standards,  a  slum  lacks  at  least  one  of  the  four  essentials  of  a  healthy  house:  ○  Access  to  safe  water.  ○   Access  to  sanitation.  ○   Safe  and  secure  tenure.  ○   Durable  housing.  ●  A  Slum  area  is  characterized  by  extreme  squalor  and  overcrowding.   ○  Poor  housing  creates  a  cascade  of  risks.  Overcrowding  makes  it  impossible  to  maintain  hygiene.  Poor  ang  hygiene  leads  to  diseases.  Additionally,  living  in  an  overcrowded  environment  will  
create  massive  psychological  stress.    
F.  PROBLEMS  ASSOCIATED  WITH   POOR  HOUSING  
 ●  Inadequate  Lighting  ○  Leads  to  accidents  and  hinders  cleanliness;  few  dwellings  maximize  natural  light.  ○  Light  is  essential  for  hygiene,  making  us  aware  of  the  mess  we  have  in  our  surroundings.  ●  HIgher  Stress  ○  Poverty  and  poor  housing  reduce  the  body's  ability  to  fight  infection.  ○  Residents  will  be  more  susceptible  to  pathogens.  ●  Chronic  Illness  ○  Environmental  stress  accelerates  the  deterioration  of  existing  conditions.   ●  Food  and  Water  Contamination  ○  Poor  hygiene  leads  to  household  contamination.  Pathogens  flourish  when  there  are  no  proper  facilities  for  washing  hands  or  cleaning  food.  ○  Common  pathogens:  Salmonella,  Staphylococcus,  Streptococcus.  ○  Impact:  Higher  infant  mortality,  poorer  nutritional  health.  ●  Respiratory  and  Eyesight  Problems  ○  Poor  Indoor  Air  Quality:  Contaminants  are  often  higher  indoors  than  outdoors  due  to  a  lack  of  dispersion.  ■  Usually,  in  slums,  residents  prioritize  roofs  over  ventilation.  This  leads  to  improper  ventilation  and  humid  environments,  causing  mold  to  grow.

●  II.            OCCUPATIONAL  ENVIRONMENT   
●  " The  real  enemy  of  safety  is  not  non-compliance  but  non-thinking.”  -  Rob  Long  ●  True  occupational  safety  requires  critical  thinking  and  the  active  anticipation  of  risks.  
A.  DEFINITION  OF  TERMS  
●  Occupational  Health:  Focuses  on  well-being  and  illness  prevention;  professionals  are  "Industrial  Hygienists".  ●  Occupational  Safety:  Focuses  on  injury  prevention;  professionals  are  "Safety  Engineers".  ●  OSH:  Science  of  anticipation,  recognition,  evaluation,  and  control  of  workplace  hazards.  ●  Health  and  safety  are  closely  related  but  distinct  concepts.  Health  focuses  on  long-term  well-being  and  the  prevention  of  illnesses,  while  safety  is  concerned  with  preventing  immediate  accidents  and  injuries.  By  addressing  both  health  and  safety  together,  we  aim  to  protect  not  only  workers  but  also  the  surrounding  community  and  the  environment.  
B.  THE  AIM  OF  OCCUPATIONAL  HEALTH  
●  International  Labor  Organization  (ILO/WHO)  Joint  Committee  Definition:  “The  promotion  and  maintenance  of  the  highest  degree  of  physical,  mental,  and  social  well-being  of  workers  in  all  occupations”.  ●  Requirements:  ○  Safe  and  healthy  work  environments.  ○  Access  to  occupational  health  services  for  all.  
C.  RIGHT  TO  WORK  
●   Universal  Declaration  of  Human  Rights  (Article  23):  ○   Every  human  has  the  right  to  work.  ○   Right  to  a  fair  wage.  ○   Right  to  join  a  trade  union.

D.  BRIEF  HISTORY  
●   4  B.C.  ○  Hippocrates  described  lead  poisoning  in  miners   ●  1556  ○  Agricola  noted  diseases  and  accidents  among  miners  ●  1567  ○  Paracelsus  describes  smelter  worker  diseases.  ●  1700s  ○  Ramazzini  examines  health  conditions  of  50+  occupations.  ■  father  of  occupational  medicine  ●  1802  ○  Health  and  Morals  of  Apprentices  Act  ■  The  Industrial  Revolution  was  a  time  when  we  saw  an  increased  number  of  people  coming  to  the  cities  because  there  was  a  higher  number  of  jobs  being  offered.  This  led  to  overcrowding  and  diseases  due  to  poor  hygiene  practices.  This  problem  led  to  the  establishment  of  the  Act.  ●  1919  ○  Creation  of  the  International  Labour  Organization  (ILO)  ■  Philosophy:  Prevention  and  protection  through  international  standards  ■  Global  necessity   ●  1970  (OSH  in  the  United  States)  ○  The  Occupational  Safety  and  Health  Act  (OSH  Act)  was  a  turning  point.  Created  two  distinct  bodies:  
■  Occupational  Safety  and  Health  Association  (OSHA)  ■  National  Institute  for  Occupational  Safety  and  Health  (NIOSH)    ○  OSHA:  Enforces  standards  through  training  and  education.  ○  NIOSH:  A  research  body  that  develops  and  recommends  standards.  ●  NIOSH  and  Research  ○  NIOSH:  U.S.  federal  agency  for  OSH  research.  ■  Responsible  for  related  research,  education,  and  training  programs.  ■  Data  used  to  develop  safety  standards  and  hazard  evaluations.  It  is  their  data  that  helps  set  the  threshold  limit  values  or  the  exposure  limits.  (eg.  limit  of  chemical  exposure  in  the  workplace.)  ●  NIOSH  vs.  OSHA  ○  NIOSH  (The  Watchdog):  Focuses  on  research  and  chronic  health  issues;  identifies  chemicals  or  diseases  harming  workers.  ○  OSHA  (The  Regulator):  Enforces  safety  and  adopts  new  standards  based  on  NIOSH  data.  ●  Collaboration:  NIOSH  identifies  the  need  for  change;  OSHA  implements  it.         
E.  OCCUPATIONAL  HEALTH  AND  SAFETY  IN  THE  PHILIPPINES   
 Philippine  OSH:  Republic  Act  No.  11058   Occupational  Safety  and  Health  Standards  Act  ●  Puts  the  primary  responsibility  on  the  employer.  Employers  are  legally  required  to  provide  appropriate  safety  equipment ,  such  as  helmets  and  masks.  They  must  also  ensure  that  all  workers  are  properly  trained  on  the  hazards  they  may  encounter.  For  any  machine  or  equipment  used,  workers  should  receive  adequate  training  beforehand.    Employee  Rights  and  Duties:  ●  Right  to  know  inherent  risks  ●  Right  to  refuse  unsafe  work  ●  Duty  to  report  accidents  ●  Active  participation  in  safety  planning  
F.   HAZARD  vs  INJURY  
●  Hazard:  Something  with  the  potential  to  cause  harm  (potential  hazard)  ●  Occupational  Hazard:  Risk  experienced  specifically  in  the  workplace.  ●  Goal:  Identifying  hazards  before  they  cause  harm  allows  for  prevention

H.  THE  OSH  MANAGEMENT  CYCLE  
●  Policy :  OSH  policy  and  participation  ○  Involve  the  workers  when  making  the  policy.  ●  Organizing :  Accountability  and  training  ●  Planning :  Initial  review  and  implementation  ●  Evaluation :  Audit  and  management  review  ●  Improvement :  Continual  corrective  action  
I.  OCCUPATIONAL  HEALTH  SURVEILLANCE   
●  Purpose :  Early  identification  of  dangers  before  they  cause  incurable  disease.  ○  It  is  to  make  sure  we  have  an  Early  Warning  System  (EWS).  ○  Conducting  regular  medical  exams  for  the  workers  to  catch  problems  when  they  are  still  beatable .  ●  Methods :  ○  Environmental  assessment:  Identifying  factors  in  the  workspace  ○  Biological  monitoring:  Testing  blood/urine  for  chemical  exposure  ○  Medical  surveillance:  Ongoing  health  exams  for  workers  
J.  ROLE  OF  OCCUPATIONAL  HEALTH  SERVICES  
●  Advisory  Role:  Advise  on  workplace  design  and  choice  of  machinery  ●  Monitoring :  Monitor  worker  health  in  relation  to  their  specific  tasks  ●  Response :  Organize  first  aid  and  emergency  treatment  ●  Adaptation :  Try  to  ensure  the  work  is  adapted  to  the  worker  
●  These  are  the  people  who  are  doing  the  health  surveillance:  ○  Internal  Consultants:  They’re  not  just  treating  the  injuries,  but  they  also  participate  in  the  design  of  the  floor  of  the  factory  to  ensure  that  every  machine  and  every  task  is  as  safe  as  possible.  
K.  MULTIDISCIPLINARY  TEAMS  
●  In  the  case  of  laboratories,  the  following  are  included:  ○  Medical  Technologists  ○  Pathologist  ○  Director  ●  Composition:  Occupational  physicians,  hygienists,  ergonomists,  and  OSH  nurses  (in  the  case  of  larger  organizations)  ○  Staff  must  enjoy  professional  independence  from  employers  and  workers  ○  It  takes  18  people,  all  of  whom  must  be  free  to  make  unbiased  recommendations.  They  have  the  freedom  of  making  as  harsh  a  comment  as  possible  without  getting  fired,  as  long  as  those  comments  are  directed  toward  occupational,  safety,  or  health.

III.  RECREATIONAL  ENVIRONMENT  
●  "People  who  cannot  find  time  for  recreation  are  obliged  sooner  or  later  to  find  time  for  illness."-  John  Wanamaker  ●  Recreation  is  essential  for  stress  moderation  and  weight  control.  However,  if  these  inventions  aren’t  fully  managed,  they  become  leading  grounds  for  injury  and  diseases.  
●  Recreation  ○  activities  chosen  during  leisure  time  to  refresh  the  mind  and  body.  ●  Leisure  Time  ○  Time  when  one  is  not  working.  ●  Recreation  Area  ○  Land  and  water  areas  dedicated  to  public  enjoyment    ○  It  is  a  vital  part  of  public  health  because  it  involves  providing  an  emotional  release  from  the  day-to-day  stresses  of  home  and  work.  It  helps  us  maintain  our  mental  health,  but  only  if  the  area  itself  is  sanitary  and  safe .  ●  Types  of  Recreational  Areas  ○  Parks  and  campgrounds  ○  Picnic  areas  and  resorts  ○  Hotels  and  cabin  camps   ○  Swimming,  hunting,  and  touring  facilities  ■  Each  type  of  recreational  area  can  provide  environmental  health  challenges.  (e.g.,  waste  disposals,  pool  sanitation)  ●  Recreational  Activities  and  Facilities   ○  Facilities  for  activities  like:  ■  Swimming  ■  Fishing  ■  Hunting  ■  Boating  ■  Sailing  ■  Hiking  ■  Picnicking  ■  Camping  ■  Sightseeing   
A.  RECREATIONAL  AREAS  
●  Indoor  ○  Enclosed  Spaces:  ■  Accommodate  a  limited  number  of  people.  ■  They  allow  for  control  of  air  quality  and  temperature.  ○  Management:   ■  Easier  to  control  and  maintain   ○  Examples:  Gymnasiums,  Indoor  pools,  cinemas   ●  Outdoor  ○  Open  Spaces:  ■  Harder  to  control  due  to  size  and  natural  variables.  ■  It  is  safer  in  terms  of  air  quality  due  to  exposure  to  fresh  air.  ○  Venue  Risks:  ■  Recreation  and  sports  venues  are  actually  more  common  sites  for  injury  than  schools  or  workplaces.   ○  Examples:  Parks,  Dive  spots,  Birdwatching  Areas,  Zoos
