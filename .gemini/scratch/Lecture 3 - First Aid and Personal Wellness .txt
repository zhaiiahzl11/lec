I.  UNDERSTANDING  STRESS                           
 Stress   ●  aka  20th  Century  Syndrome  by  Benjamin  Franklin   ●  nonspecific  response  of  the  body  to  any  demand  made  upon  it  ●  degree  to  which  you  feel  overwhelmed  or  unable  to  cope   ●  a  feeling  of  emotional  or  physical  tension  ●  can  come  from  an  event  or  thought  that  makes  you  feel  angry,  nervous,  or  frustrated   STRESS  VS.  ANXIETY   Stress   ●  caused  by  existing  stress-causing  factor  or  stressors   Anxiety   ●  Stress  continues  even  when  the  stressor  is  already  gone  ●  Long-term  effect  of  consistent  stress       
A.  ACUTE  VS.  CHRONIC  STRESS  
 2  FORMS  OF  STRESS   Acute  Stress   ●  most  common  type   ●  short-term  response  to  an  imminent  danger  or  event  ●  helps  manage  dangerous  situations  ●  Can  occur  when  you  do  something  new  or  exciting  ○  Ex:  immediate  slam  on  the  breaks  as  reflex  ;  skiing  a  steep  slope  ○   “Mini  heart  attack  feeling”   Chronic  Stress   ●  most  serious  type  of  stress  ●  continuous  or  prolonged  type   ●  more  than  an  individual  can  cope  or  control  ○  You  can  become  so  used  to  chronic  stress  that  you  do  not  realize  it  is  a  problem  and  may  affect  health.   Physiologic  Effects  of  Stress   ●  Body  reacts  to  stress  by  releasing  hormones  ●  Hormones  make  the  brain  more  alert,  cause  the  muscles  to  tense,  increase  the  pulse  ●  These  reactions  are  good  as  they  can  help  you  handle  the  situation  causing  stress;   the  body's  way  of  protecting  itself.

D.  YERKES-DODSON  LAW  
 ●  A  certain  amount  of  anxiety  can  enhance  performance  too  much  can  impair  it  (ie;  severe  stage  fright)  ●  Model  of  the  relationship  between  stress  and  task  performance  ●  Also  known  as  Inverted  U  Model  of  Arousal  –  depicted  as  an  upside  down  u-shaped  curve  ●  Was  originally  developed  by  psychologists  Robert  Yerkes  and  John  Dillingham  Dodson  in  1908  ○  Horizontal  line  (Stress)  :  a.  Left  side  =  low  arousal/  stress  b.  Middle  =  medium  arousal  c.  Right  side  =  high  arousal   ○  Vertical  line  (Performance)  :  a.  Bottom  =  low  performance  b.  Middle  =  medium  performance  c.  Upper  =  high  performance  
    
E.  GENERAL  ADAPTATION  SYNDROME  (GAS)  
 ●  Developed  by  Dr.  Hans  Selye  ●  Three  stage  process  that  describes  the  physiological  changes  that  the  body  goes  through  when  it  is  under  stress   THREE-STAGE  PROCESS  OF  STRESS  RESPONSE   Alarm  Reaction  Stage   ●  initial  symptoms  the  body  experiences  when  under  stress   ●  “fight-or-flight”  response   ●  increase  in  heart  rate,  blood  pressure,  respiration  rate   ●  blood  sugar  levels  rise,  muscles  tense  up,  pupils  dilate,  and  perspiration  increases  ●  The  adrenal  gland  releases  cortisol ,  a  stress-hormone,  and  a  boost  of  adrenaline  that  increases  energy   Stage  of  Resistance   ●  not  as  intense  as  the  alarm  reaction  stage  but  the  bodily  levels  of  hormonal  secretion  are  still  above  normal   ●  Autonomic  Nervous  System  (ANS)  tries  to  return  the  body  to  normal  by  reducing  the  amount  of  cortisol  produced   ●  heart  rate  and  blood  pressure  begin  to  return  to  normal  ●  If  the  stressor  remains,  the  body  will  stay  in  a  state  of  alert  and  stress  hormones  continue  to  be  produced  ○  Signs:  irritability,  frustration,  and  poor  concentration

A.  BASIC  LIFE  SUPPORT  
 ●  Involves  essential  emergency  procedures  to  maintain  breathing  and  circulation  in  a  person  experiencing  cardiac  arrest  or  respiratory  failure.  ●  Includes  CPR  (chest  compressions  and  rescue  breaths),  using  an  automated  external  defibrillator  (AED),  and  clearing  airway  obstructions  
●  Aims  to  sustain  life  until  professional  medical  help  arrives,  improving  survival  chances.  
In  a  life  and  death  situation,  actions   must  be  taken  to  ensure   that  lives  can  be  saved.  
3  IMPORTANT  STEPS! 
Step  1:  Secure  the  scene  
-  This  means  pulling  the  victim  out  of  dangerous  situations ,  e.g.  standing  water,  traffic,  and  other  dangerous  situations.  -  ENSURE  THAT  YOU,  YOURSELF  ARE  SAFE  BEFORE  PERFORMING  BASIC  LIFE  SUPPORT  
Step  2:  Check  for  a  response  
-  Shake  shoulders  gently  -  Ask  “are  you  all  right?”  or  other  types  of  questions  that  may  induce  a  response  -  If  they  respond:  ○  Leave  as  you  find  them  (DON’T  touch  them,  not  leave  as  if  biyaan)  ○  Find  out  what  is  wrong  ○  Reassess  regularly   -  IF  the  victim  is  UNRESPONSIVE,   and  has  abnormal  respirations  ○  no  respiration  or  gasping/agonal  (Shallow,  slow,  and  infrequent  gasps)  breathing  ○  activate  EMS  (emergency  medical  services)   -  yell  for  help  ○  send  someone  for  an  AED  (automatic  external  defibrillator)  ○   use  a  cell  phone  if  one  is  available  
Step  3:  Check  for  a  pulse  
-  Check  for  a  pulse  at  the  carotid  artery  and  check  breathing  -  Check  no  longer  than  10  second  -   If  there  is  a  pulse  and  breathing,  continue  to  monitor  and  support  breathing  -  if  there  is  a  pulse  and  no/abnormal  breathing,  start  rescue  breathing   
*If  there  is  no  pulse  and  no/abnormal  breathing:  Start  High  Quality  CPR  
 
B.  CPR  
 ●  A  life-saving  technique  used  in  emergencies  when  a  person’s  breathing  or  heartbeat  has  stopped.  ●  Involves  chest  compressions  (pushing  hard  and  fast  in  the  center  of  the  chest)  and  rescue  breaths  to  restore  circulation  and  oxygen  flow.  ●  Increases  survival  chances  by  maintaining  blood  flow  to  vital  organs  until  professional  medical  help  arrives. CPR-ADULT  AND  CHILD    CPR  (Cardiopulmonary  Resuscitation)  
 
●
 
Emergency  procedure  used  when  breathing  or  heartbeat  has  stopped.
 
 Purpose:  -  Maintain  blood  circulation  -  Provide  oxygen  to  the  brain  and  vital  organs  -  Prevent  brain  damage  -  Brain  damage  can  begin  within  4–6  minutes  without  oxygen.  
When  to  Perform  CPR?   Person  is:  -  Unresponsive  -  Not  breathing  or  only  gasping  -  Has  no  pulse  (for  trained  responders)   Initial  Response  Steps  1.  Check  for  Responsiveness  -  Tap  shoulders  and  shout:  “Are  you  okay?”  -  If  no  response  →  proceed  immediately.  2.  Call  for  Help  -  Activate  emergency  response  system.  -  Call  the  local  emergency  number.  -  Ask  someone  to  get  an  AED  (Automated  External  Defibrillator)  if  available  
 
3.  Check  Breathing  -  Look  for  normal  breathing  (5–10  seconds).   -  Occasional  gasps  are  NOT  normal  breathing.   
ADULT  CPR 
1.  Handplacement  
●  Place  the  heel  of  on  one  hand  on:  -  Center  of  the  chest  -  Lower  half  of  the  sternum  ●  Place  second  hand  on  top:  -  Interlocked  fingers  -  Keep  arms  straight  -  Shoulders  directly  above  hands   2.  Chest  Compressions  ●  Depth:  At  least  2  inches  (5cm)  ●  Rate:  100-120  compressions  per  minute  ●  Allow  full  chest  to  recoil  ●  Minimize  interruptions   3.  Compression-To-  Breath  Ratio  ●  30  compressions:  2  breaths  ●  Continue  Cycle  until:  -  AED  arrives  -   Professional  help  arrives  -  Person  shows  signs  of  life   -  You  are  too  exhausted  to  continue  4.  Rescue  Breaths  ●  Open  airway  using:  -  Head-tilt,  chin-lift  -  Pinch  nose  closed.  -  Give  1  breath  over  1  second.  -  Chest  should  visibly  rise.  -  Give  second  breath.  -  Avoid  excessive  ventilation.
