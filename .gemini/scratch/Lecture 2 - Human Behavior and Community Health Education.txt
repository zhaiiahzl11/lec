I.  BEHAVIOR  
 Behavior   ●  Externally  visible  activity  of  an  animal,  in  which  a  coordinated  pattern  of  sensory,  motor,  and  associated  neural  activity  responds  to  changing  external  or  internal  conditions."  (Beck,  et.  al,  1981)  ●  A  response  to  external  and  internal  stimuli  following  the  integration  of  (1)  sensory,  (2)  neural,  (3)  endocrine,  and  (4)  effector  components.  ●  “Behavior  has  a  genetic  basis;  hence  is  subject  to  natural  selection,  and  it  can  be  commonly  modified  through  experience.”  (Starr  and  Taggart,  1992)  ●  “Behavior  is  a  Observable  activity  of  an  organism;  anything  an  organism  does  that  
involves  action  and/or  response  to  stimulation."  (Wallace  et  al.,  1991)  ●  In  simpler  terms,  behavior  is  how  someone  acts  and  a  response  to  things  that  are  happening.  Internally,  thoughts  and  feelings  while  externally   ●  refer  to  the  relations  and  environment  around  us.  It  is  what  a  person  does  to  make  something  happen.  It  is  what  a  person  does  to  make  something  change  or  it  is  what  a  person  does  to  keep  things  the  same.

B.  VIEWS  ON  HUMAN  BEHAVIOR  
  
Biological  Viewpoint  Psychiatrist’s  Point  of  View   ●  Everything  you  think  and  feel  is  controlled  by  electrical  and  chemical  activity  in  your  brain  and  the  rest  of  your  body.  ●  To  these  theorists,  the  body  controls  the  mind  and  not  the  other  way  around.   Intrapsychic  Viewpoint  Psychologist’s  Point  of  View   ●  The  psychologists  try  to  explain  the  behaviors  they’ve  measured  in  terms  of  mental  processes  such  as  perceptions,  motives,  values,  attitudes,  and  memories.   ●  The  mind  dominates  most  bodily  activities  ●  This  viewpoint  is  opposite  to  the  biological  viewpoint   ●  e.g.  The  psychologist  will  try  to  teach  a  depressed  person  to  stop  thinking  about  himself/herself  in  negative  ways  and  encourage  him/her  to  set  realistic  positive  goals.  Social/Behavioral  Viewpoint   
●  One’s  behavior  can  be  explained  in  terms  of   their  physical,  social,  and  cultural  environment.  ●  One  is  not  an  island  unto  oneself;  he  or  she  grew  up  around  people  who  help  determine  his  or  her  ideas,  values,  speech,  and  behaviors.   ●  E.g.  A  person  who  is  depressed  and  was  caused  by  a  broken  relationship,  making  her  feel  unspecial  because  she  was  built  up  by  afather  who  gived  her  special  attention  and  special  treatment

A.  HEALTH  BELIEF  MODEL  
 Health  Belief  Model   ●  Developed  by  a  group  of  psychologists  to  explain  why  the  public  failed  to  participate  in  tuberculosis  (Hochbaum,  1958)  ●  Through  their  work,  this  group  found  that  information  alone  is  rarely  enough  to  motivate  one  to  act  –  individuals  must  know  what  to  do  and  how  to  do  it  before  they  can  take  action  and  the  information  must  be  related  to  one’s  needs  ●  It  is  based  on  the  assumption  that  the  major  determinant  of  preventive  health  behavior  is  disease  avoidance.  ●  Limitation :  it  assumes  that  only  those  clients  who  have  distorted  or  negative  perceptions  of  the  disease  or  recommended  health  action  will  fail  to  act.  
Concept  Definition  
Perceived  susceptibility   
●  One’s  belief  regarding  the  chance  of  getting  a  given  condition  ●  Refers  to  a  person’s  subjective  perception  of  a  risk  of  acquiring  an  illness  or  disease  
●  There  is  a  wide  variation  in  a  person’s  feelings  of  a  person’s  personal  vulnerability  of  a  disease   
Perceived  severity   
●  One’s  belief  regarding  the  seriousness  of  a  given  condition  ●  One’s  belief  regarding  the  seriousness  of  a  given  condition  ●  Refers  on  a  person’s  feelings  on  the  seriousness  of  contracting  an  illness  or  disease  ●  There  is  a  wide  variation  in  a  person’s  feelings  of  severity  and  often  a  person  considers  the  medical  (death)  or  social  (relationship  with  others)  consequences    
Perceived  benefits  
●  One’s  belief  in  the  ability  of  an  advised  action  to  reduce  the  health  risk  or  seriousness  of  a  given  condition  ●  Refers  to  a  person’s  perception  of  the  effectiveness  of  various  actions  available  to  reduce  the  threat  of  illness  or  disease  ●  The  course  of  action  takes  in  preventing  the  illness  or  disease  relies  in  the  consideration  and  evaluation  of  both  perceived  susceptibility  and  benefit  such  that  the  
person  would  accept  the  recommended  action  if  it  was  perceived  beneficial   
Perceived  barriers    
●  One’s  belief  regarding  the  tangible  and  psychological  costs  of  an  advised  action  ●  Refers  to  a  person’s  feelings  on  the  obstacles  to  performing  the  recommended  health  action  ●  There  is  a  wide  variation  in  a  person’s  feelings  of  barriers  or  impediments  which  can  lead  to  a  cost  or  benefit  analysis  ●  The  person  weighs  the  effectiveness  of  an  action  against  the  perceptions  that  it  may  be  expensive,  dangerous  or  side  effects  that  he/she  may  feel.
